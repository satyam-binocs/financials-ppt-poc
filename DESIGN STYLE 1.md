# Editable PowerPoint Business Deck — Design & Rendering Skill

## 1. Core Design Objective

Create a premium, editable PowerPoint business deck inspired by the visual discipline of top-tier strategy consulting firms, Big Four advisory firms, investment committees, and corporate strategy presentations.

The presentation must look:

- Executive-level
- Analytical
- Structured
- Clean
- Premium
- Business-oriented
- Professionally designed
- Highly editable in PowerPoint

The output must not look like:

- A generic AI-generated presentation
- A decorated Canva slide
- A startup pitch deck
- A collection of random cards
- An overly colorful infographic
- A template with excessive decoration
- A slide filled with unnecessary visual elements

Prioritize business storytelling, information hierarchy, evidence, clarity, and disciplined composition over decoration.

---

## 2. Design Philosophy

Treat every slide as an information architecture problem first and a visual styling problem second.

Every slide should answer:

1. What is the main message?
2. What evidence supports that message?
3. What should the audience notice first?
4. What is the implication or takeaway?
5. How can the information be presented with the least visual noise?

Use conclusion-led headlines wherever possible.

Instead of generic titles such as:

- Revenue Analysis
- EBITDA
- Market Overview

Prefer insight-led titles such as:

- Revenue growth was primarily driven by the Stratosphere segment
- EBITDA margins remained stable despite revenue expansion
- Management's plan implies a meaningful upside versus the base case

The title should communicate the slide's conclusion whenever the source data supports one.

---

## 3. Overall Visual Character

Use a refined consulting/business-deck style with:

- White or very light neutral backgrounds
- Strong dark typography
- Restrained corporate colors
- Clear primary and secondary colors
- Minimal decoration
- Strong alignment
- Consistent spacing
- Editorial composition
- Analytical charts
- Structured tables
- Balanced information density
- Subtle section hierarchy
- Professional footnotes and sources

The deck should feel like it was created by an experienced strategy consultant, financial analyst, or presentation specialist.

---

## 4. Color System

Use a consistent color system across the entire presentation.

### Primary Color

Deep Navy / Dark Blue

Use for:

- Main headlines
- Important labels
- Primary data series
- Key figures
- Major structural elements

### Secondary Color

Corporate Blue

Use for:

- Secondary data series
- Supporting highlights
- Chart comparisons
- Selected emphasis
- Section-level visual differentiation

### Supporting Color

Slate Gray / Cool Gray

Use for:

- Secondary text
- Axis labels
- Supporting descriptions
- Borders
- Footnotes
- Less important information

### Accent Color

Use only one restrained accent color per deck, such as:

- Muted Teal
- Muted Gold
- Desaturated Green
- Muted Orange

Use the accent only for:

- Important callouts
- Positive/negative variance
- One key data point
- Strategic emphasis
- Small visual markers

Do not use many accent colors simultaneously.

### Background

Use:

- White
- Very light cool gray
- Extremely subtle neutral backgrounds

Avoid:

- Dark full-slide backgrounds unless explicitly required
- Heavy tinted panels
- Strong gradients
- Bright colored backgrounds
- Decorative background patterns

---

## 5. Background Rules

The default slide background should be clean and mostly white.

Do not automatically add:

- Large colored background blocks
- Full-width decorative banners
- Heavy tinted panels
- Gradient backgrounds
- Background illustrations
- Decorative textures
- Oversized shapes behind content

Background elements should only be used when they improve hierarchy or grouping.

---

## 6. Shadow Rules

### Default Rule: NO SHADOWS

Do not use:

- Drop shadows
- Card shadows
- Floating panel shadows
- Soft glow effects
- 3D effects
- Neumorphism
- Heavy elevation effects

The deck should look flat, precise, editorial, and consulting-grade.

Use spacing, borders, alignment, typography, and subtle fills to create hierarchy instead of shadows.

---

## 7. Decorative Line Rules

Do not automatically add decorative lines.

Avoid:

- Unnecessary horizontal lines below titles
- Lines below subtitles
- Random top accent bars
- Decorative separators
- Multiple divider lines on one slide
- Lines that do not communicate structure

Specifically:

- Do not automatically place a horizontal divider below the main title or subtitle.
- Do not add a top accent line unless it serves a clear structural purpose.
- Do not use lines merely to make the slide look “designed.”

Use borders only when they clarify:

- Table structure
- Section grouping
- Column separation
- KPI grouping
- Content hierarchy

---

## 8. Typography System

Use a modern, professional sans-serif font such as:

- Aptos
- Arial
- Helvetica
- Calibri
- Inter
- A similar clean business font

Maintain a clear hierarchy.

### Suggested Hierarchy

- Slide title: Large, bold, dark navy
- Subtitle/context: Smaller, muted gray
- Section label: Small uppercase or compact label
- KPI value: Large and bold
- KPI label: Small, restrained, gray
- Body text: Medium size, highly readable
- Footnotes/source: Small but legible

Avoid:

- Too many font families
- Excessive bolding
- Decorative fonts
- Very thin text
- Overly condensed typography
- Inconsistent text sizes

Typography should communicate hierarchy before color does.

---

## 9. Slide Grid System

Use a consistent 16:9 widescreen layout.

Reference dimensions:

- Width: 13.333 inches
- Height: 7.5 inches

Use a disciplined grid:

- Consistent left and right margins
- Consistent top title position
- Consistent footer position
- Consistent column spacing
- Consistent content baselines
- Consistent alignment across slides

Prefer a 12-column conceptual grid or equivalent structured layout.

All major objects should align to a shared grid.

Avoid arbitrary placement.

---

## 10. Layout Composition

Each slide should have a clear composition.

Typical structure:

1. Small section label, if required
2. Conclusion-led title
3. Optional short context line
4. Main evidence area
5. Supporting interpretation or takeaway
6. Source/footer

Use a strong visual hierarchy:

- Primary message
- Supporting evidence
- Secondary context
- Footnote/source

Do not make every element equally prominent.

---

## 11. Content Density

Use executive-level information density.

The slide should be:

- Dense enough to be useful
- Sparse enough to remain readable
- Structured enough to scan quickly

Avoid:

- Excessive empty space with no purpose
- Overcrowded slides
- Long paragraphs
- Repeated information
- Tiny unreadable text
- Too many separate cards
- Unnecessary decorative components

If content is dense, improve structure rather than simply shrinking the font.

---

## 12. Card Design Rules

Cards may be used selectively, but do not turn every piece of information into a card.

Use cards only when they create meaningful grouping, such as:

- KPI summaries
- Scenario comparisons
- Key findings
- Segment summaries
- Risk/implication blocks

Card styling should be:

- Flat
- Minimal
- Lightly bordered or subtly filled
- Consistently aligned
- Consistent in padding
- Free of shadows

Avoid:

- Excessive rounded cards
- Floating card layouts
- Cards with unnecessary icons
- Cards with heavy backgrounds
- Random card sizes
- Too many cards on one slide

---

## 13. Table Design

Tables must look like professional consulting or financial-analysis tables.

Use:

- Strong header hierarchy
- Dark or navy header text
- Subtle row separators
- Consistent numeric alignment
- Right-aligned numbers
- Consistent decimal formatting
- Proper units
- Clear column labels
- Minimal borders
- Highlighting only where meaningful

Avoid:

- Heavy gridlines around every cell
- Bright colored table fills
- Excessive alternating colors
- Inconsistent number formats
- Center-aligning financial values unnecessarily
- Tiny table text

Use bold selectively for:

- Totals
- Key rows
- Important metrics
- Final scenario values

---

## 14. Chart Design

Charts must be native editable PowerPoint charts wherever possible.

Do not flatten charts into images unless absolutely unavoidable.

Charts should be:

- Simple
- Analytical
- Easy to read
- Properly labeled
- Consistent with the deck palette
- Free of unnecessary decoration

Use:

- Clean axes
- Minimal gridlines
- Direct labels when practical
- Consistent number formatting
- Restrained colors
- Clear legends
- Appropriate chart titles or explanatory subtitles

Avoid:

- 3D charts
- Excessive gridlines
- Pie charts with too many slices
- Decorative chart backgrounds
- Unnecessary data labels everywhere
- Rainbow color schemes
- Heavy chart borders
- Unclear legends

---

## 15. Chart Selection Rules

Choose the chart based on the analytical question.

Use:

- Column/bar chart for comparisons
- Line chart for trends over time
- Stacked column/bar for composition
- Waterfall for bridge analysis
- Scatter plot for relationships
- Donut/pie only for simple composition with very few categories
- Table when exact values matter more than visual pattern
- KPI block when only one or two numbers matter

Do not force a chart when a table or KPI is clearer.

---

## 16. KPI Design

KPI blocks should communicate the most important numbers immediately.

Each KPI should generally contain:

- Metric label
- Main value
- Optional change/variance
- Optional short descriptor

Use:

- Large bold number
- Small muted label
- Clear unit
- Consistent alignment
- Minimal separators

Avoid:

- Large decorative icons
- Circular gauges
- Excessive color
- Shadows
- Multiple competing highlights
- Unnecessary containers

---

## 17. Insight Callouts

Use insight callouts to explain what the data means.

A good insight callout should contain:

- A concise takeaway
- Optional supporting number
- Optional implication

Example structure:

**Key implication:**  
The majority of revenue growth was concentrated in one segment, increasing concentration dependency.

Callouts should be visually distinct but restrained.

Use:

- Small accent marker
- Bold lead-in
- Light background if necessary
- Strong alignment

Avoid oversized quote boxes or decorative speech bubbles.

---

## 18. Footer System

Use a consistent footer across slides.

Footer may include:

- Source
- Data period
- Confidentiality label
- Page number
- Small contextual note

Keep it:

- Small
- Muted
- Aligned
- Consistent
- Legible

Do not let footers compete with the main content.

---

## 19. Section Labels

Use section labels sparingly.

Examples:

- HISTORICAL PERFORMANCE
- REVENUE MIX
- EARNINGS QUALITY
- FORWARD OUTLOOK

Section labels should be:

- Small
- Uppercase or compact
- Muted or secondary blue
- Consistent across slides

Do not make section labels larger than the slide title.

---

## 20. Slide Archetypes

Use the appropriate layout based on the content.

### A. Executive Summary Slide

Structure:

- Conclusion-led title
- 3–5 key findings
- Supporting KPI or mini-chart
- Clear takeaway

### B. KPI Overview Slide

Structure:

- Large KPI row
- Supporting context
- One analytical visual or summary panel

### C. Trend Analysis Slide

Structure:

- Main trend chart
- Supporting commentary
- Highlighted inflection point

### D. Segment Analysis Slide

Structure:

- Segment comparison chart/table
- Mix or contribution analysis
- Key implication

### E. Margin/Earnings Quality Slide

Structure:

- Margin trend
- EBITDA bridge or comparison
- Add-back/quality commentary

### F. Scenario Analysis Slide

Structure:

- Base case
- Management plan
- Upside/downside
- Variance or gap visualization

### G. Data Table Slide

Structure:

- Clear table
- Highlighted key rows
- Short interpretation
- Source

Do not use the same card-grid layout for every slide.

---

## 21. Information Hierarchy

The viewer should understand the slide in this order:

1. Main conclusion
2. Most important number or chart
3. Supporting evidence
4. Secondary details
5. Source/footnote

If everything is bold, colored, boxed, or highlighted, nothing is important.

Use emphasis selectively.

---

## 22. Editability Requirements

All important content must remain editable in PowerPoint.

Prefer native PowerPoint objects:

- Text boxes
- Shapes
- Tables
- Native charts
- Editable lines
- Editable labels
- Editable icons only when necessary

Do not use flattened screenshots as the primary slide content.

Do not convert the whole slide into one image.

Maintain:

- Editable text
- Editable chart data
- Editable table cells
- Editable shapes
- Editable colors
- Editable positioning

---

## 23. Content Preservation

Do not lose, omit, or silently rewrite source information.

Preserve:

- All numerical values
- All labels
- All categories
- All periods
- All scenario values
- All source notes
- All important explanatory text
- Original data relationships
- Original meaning and structure

You may improve hierarchy and visual presentation, but do not remove meaningful information merely to make the slide cleaner.

If content is too dense, restructure it intelligently.

---

## 24. Overflow Management

If content does not fit:

1. Reorganize the layout
2. Reduce unnecessary spacing
3. Adjust column widths
4. Split content into logical sections
5. Use a second slide if necessary
6. Reduce font size moderately only as a last resort

Never allow:

- Text overflow
- Object overlap
- Clipped text
- Hidden values
- Broken tables
- Chart collisions
- Misaligned labels
- Content extending beyond slide boundaries

Do not solve overflow by making everything unreadably small.

---

## 25. Alignment Rules

Ensure:

- Titles align consistently
- Charts align with tables
- KPI blocks share baselines
- Columns have equal or intentional widths
- Text boxes use consistent left edges
- Numeric values align properly
- Footers align consistently
- Margins are visually balanced

Use alignment and distribution tools conceptually even when generating programmatically.

---

## 26. Spacing Rules

Use consistent spacing between:

- Section label and title
- Title and content
- Chart and commentary
- KPI label and value
- Table sections
- Content blocks
- Footer and main content

Avoid both:

- Cramped elements
- Excessive unexplained gaps

Whitespace should improve readability and hierarchy, not look accidental.

---

## 27. Professionalism Test

Before finalizing each slide, ask:

- Does the title communicate the main point?
- Is the most important information immediately visible?
- Is the slide easy to scan?
- Are the colors restrained and consistent?
- Are all elements aligned?
- Is there unnecessary decoration?
- Are the charts analytically appropriate?
- Are tables readable?
- Is the slide editable?
- Does it look like a professional consulting deck?

---

## 28. Final Visual QA Checklist

Before exporting the PPTX, verify:

- No unnecessary shadows
- No unnecessary top accent lines
- No unnecessary divider below subtitle/title
- No excessive panel fills
- No excessive rounded cards
- No random decorative shapes
- Consistent primary, secondary, and accent colors
- Clean white or very light background
- Strong typographic hierarchy
- Editable charts and tables
- No text overflow
- No object overlap
- No clipping
- No content loss
- No inconsistent spacing
- No misaligned elements
- No unreadable small text
- No excessive visual noise
- No generic AI-template appearance
- Balanced slide density
- Clear business takeaway on every slide

---

## 29. Final Output Standard

The final output must feel like:

> A premium, editable, analyst-built consulting presentation with strong business storytelling and disciplined visual design.

It should resemble the quality and restraint of a professional:

- Strategy consulting deck
- Big Four advisory presentation
- Corporate finance analysis
- Investment committee presentation
- Board-level business review
- Executive management report

The design should be rich through:

- Typography
- Alignment
- Color hierarchy
- Analytical visuals
- Structured composition
- Spacing
- Data storytelling

Not through:

- Shadows
- Gradients
- Decorative lines
- Random icons
- Excessive cards
- Bright colors
- Unnecessary shapes

---

## 30. Critical Rendering Principle

The renderer should not blindly apply a fixed template to every slide.

It must dynamically choose the most suitable composition based on:

- Content type
- Number of data points
- Text length
- Chart requirements
- Table density
- Number of categories
- Importance of the conclusion
- Available space
- Relationship between elements

The layout engine should adapt while preserving the same overall design language.

---

## 31. Anti-Template / Anti-AI Styling Rule

Do not make every slide look identical.

Avoid repeating:

- The same three-card layout
- The same chart placement
- The same colored boxes
- The same decorative elements
- The same title treatment
- The same visual pattern regardless of content

Use a coherent design system, but allow slide-specific composition.

Every slide should feel intentionally designed for its information rather than generated from a rigid template.
